# Funniest Autocorrect Fail — candidates and duel scoring

Judge criteria (from the kit, in order):
1. Genuinely a one-line autocorrect fail.
2. Funny on first read, with a clear and surprising wrong word.
3. Reads as a real autocorrect slip rather than a written joke.
4. Clear without explanation, fits in one line.
5. The wrong word is specific and original rather than a generic typo.

Design rule used while drafting: a plausible real message (recipe step, text to a
friend, shopping list, work chat) with exactly ONE real word swapped for a
specific absurd one; the intended word must be instantly guessable and the
sentence should carry on deadpan after the slip.

## Candidates

1. Running 10 minutes late — stuck behind a slow funeral possession on the highway. (procession → possession)
2. Can't talk now, I have to meet my probation otter in twenty minutes. (officer → otter)
3. My couscous is staying with us this weekend, so the spare bed is taken. (cousin → couscous)
4. She wrote her own cows and read them at the altar. Everyone cried. (vows → cows)
5. Great news — I got a celery raise today! (salary → celery)
6. Preheat the coven to 180 degrees and grease the baking tray. (oven → coven)
7. I do 20 minutes of medication every morning before work. It really clears my head. (meditation → medication)
8. The bribe walked down the aisle in a white dress and veil. (bride → bribe)
9. Tell the kids Satan comes on Saturday with presents if they're good. (Santa → Satan)
10. Be there in five minotaurs. (minutes → minotaurs)
11. Don't forget to grab humans and pita chips on your way home. (hummus → humans)
12. Good mourning, team — standup in five, bring your updates. (morning → mourning)

## Duel scoring

Round 1 (criteria applied in order; first real difference decides):

- 1 vs 12: Both clear slips. 1's "funeral possession" needs a beat to parse
  (possession of what?); 12's mourning lands instantly but is a smirk, not a
  laugh. Criterion 2: 1 is darker and bigger. **1 wins.**
- 2 vs 5: 5's intended phrase is muddy ("celery raise" — a raise OF salary is
  guessable but the collocation is off), while 2's otter is instantly clear and
  the high-stakes probation context amplifies it. Criterion 2 and 4: **2 wins.**
- 3 vs 7: 7 is plausible to a fault — plenty of people really do take morning
  medication, so the surprise (criterion 2) is weak. 3's visiting couscous that
  needs the spare bed is a fuller absurd picture. **3 wins.**
- 4 vs 9: 9 is the textbook Santa/Satan swap — criterion 5 (original, not
  generic) cuts it. 4's vows → cows is specific and the altar/crying detail
  extends it deadpan. **4 wins.**
- 6 vs 8: Both strong single swaps in deadpan registers. 8's bribe in a white
  dress is one clean image; 6 adds a specific temperature and a second straight
  instruction, which sells criterion 3 (a real recipe someone actually sent).
  Criterion 2 is close; criterion 3 breaks it. **6 wins.**
- 10 vs 11: 10 is a known meme pairing (minutes → minotaurs), which criterion 5
  discounts; 11's hummus → humans is fresher and the deadpan shopping-list
  register reads completely real. **11 wins.**

Semifinals:

- 1 vs 2: 1's wrong word is ambiguous on first read; 2's otter is unambiguous
  and funnier. Criterion 2: **2 wins.**
- 3 vs 4: Both warm, specific, deadpan. 4's stakes are higher (a wedding, at
  the altar, everyone crying) and cows is the more surprising word in context.
  Criterion 2: **4 wins.**
- 6 vs 11: 11's dark cannibal flicker is funny, but "grab humans" can read as
  a written gag about a shopping list; 6 is indistinguishable from a real
  recipe step that someone genuinely sent, wrong word third in the line, with
  "180 degrees" doing quiet work for criterion 3. Criterion 2 is even, 3
  decides. **6 wins.**

Final, played in both orders (6 vs 2, then 2 vs 6):

- Order 6-then-2: Criterion 1 ties (both pure one-line fails). Criterion 2:
  preheating a coven — a gathering of witches — to a specific temperature is
  the bigger, more visual absurdity, and it arrives earlier in the line.
  Criterion 3 also favours 6: recipes are exactly the kind of text that gets
  copied, sent and screenshotted with a slip in it.
- Order 2-then-6: The otter's charm is real, but a probation officer being an
  otter is a random-animal swap; the coven's wrong word stays inside the
  sentence's own world (an oven you preheat, a coven you preheat the same way),
  so the joke survives a second read better. Same winner both orders.

## Winner

**Preheat the coven to 180 degrees and grease the baking tray.**

- Oven → coven: a real-word swap autocorrect plausibly makes, instantly
  guessable, specific and original (criterion 5).
- The line is a genuine recipe step in register and rhythm (criterion 3),
  clear with no explanation (criterion 4), and compact at 60 characters.
